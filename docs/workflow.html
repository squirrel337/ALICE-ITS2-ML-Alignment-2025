Section 00Orientation

The module aligns the ITS2 by attaching one small linear neural network to every sensor and training all of them simultaneously against a track-fit residual. It is a track-based alignment expressed as a learning problem rather than as a single large linear system.

Each of the 24 120 ALPIDE chips owns a YSensorCorrection object: a two-input, three-output linear network with no hidden layer. It maps the measured cluster position in the sensor frame, (s1, s2), to a correction (cs1, cs2, cs3). The corrected hits of a multi-prong event are fitted with a circle in the bending plane and a straight line in the z–β plane; the sum of normalized squared residuals is the cost. Gradients propagate back to the per-sensor weights, and an analytic Jacobian re-expresses those weights as six rigid-body alignment parameters — three rotations and three translations per sensor.

The 2025 version differs from its predecessor on one structural point above all others. The vertex that anchors the track fit is no longer read from the reconstruction and trusted; it is re-estimated from the ITS tracks themselves, at the current alignment, for every event of every epoch. Section 06 is devoted to that machinery, and Section 13 lists every other difference against the 2024 release.

Three nested repetition scales matter and are easy to confuse. An epoch is one pass over the training events inside a single job. A step is one complete job: five epochs, its own weight files, its own residual monitoring, its own archive. A campaign is a sequence of steps driven by process_all_train.sh Tag stepI stepF, in which each step is seeded from the previous step's weights. Convergence is expected across steps, not within one.

The track charge is now load-bearing

TrackData::charge is new in this version, and it is not decoration. The circle fit is seeded from the reconstructed momentum with the signed radius R = pT / q / (B2C · B), and the vertexer weights each track pair by its mean momentum. A sample whose upstream conversion leaves charge unfilled will produce a degenerate seed radius and the tracker fit will fail for every track, which the vertex stage reports as a whole-event rejection.

YDetectorGeometry is a process-wide singleton reached through the yGEOM macro. Its constructor loads the geometry, applies the starting alignment, instantiates o2::its::GeometryTGeo and fills the T2L, T2GRot and L2G matrix caches. Every coordinate transform in the module — GToS, SToG, LToG, GToL — goes through it, so the starting alignment is baked into the geometry rather than carried as an explicit offset.

The sensor frame

The module works in a sensor-local frame (s1, s2, s3) that differs from the O2 local frame and differs again between barrels. For inner-barrel chips (s1, s2, s3) = (−l3, −l1, l2) with l1 the row direction, l3 the column direction and l2 the sensor normal. Outer-barrel chips additionally flip sign according to their position within the module, which the code tracks through the per-sensor mounting direction fmntdir = ±1. In this frame s1 and s2 span the sensor plane and s3 is the normal, so a correction along s3 is a displacement out of the plane.

Section 03Beam-profile pass

run_profile_beam.C runs once per campaign, before the first step. It projects the reconstructed primary vertices into three z slices of half-width 1.5 cm, centered at z = −7.5, 0, +7.5 cm, fits a Gaussian to the x and y projection of each slice, and fits a first-order polynomial to the three resulting means. The four coefficients are written as preprocessor defines into YMLPBeamProfile.h, which is included by YMultiLayerPerceptron.cxx at compile time.

In the 2024 release there were two live routes to the beam point: the per-event reconstruction vertex, and the parameterized line these coefficients describe. In this version there is a third, and it is the one that runs. GetCost_Beam_CircleFit still builds BeamCenter(BeamPos[0], BeamPos[1], BeamPos[2]) from the event vertex and still carries the commented parameterized alternative BeamCenter(XZ1·z + XZ0, YZ1·z + YZ0, z), but immediately afterwards UseUpdatedVertexByAlignment is true and BeamCenter is overwritten with fvertex_TRKF — the vertex the module estimated for itself in Section 06.

The coefficients remain worth producing. They are the fallback for samples whose per-event vertex is unusable, they seed the longitudinal disambiguation inside the vertexer through BeamPos[2], and the slope is a direct diagnostic of beam-line quality — the values in the committed header correspond to a tilt of about 0.26 mrad in x and 0.14 mrad in y.

Section 04Data preparation

YAlignment::PrepareData(nDATA, jparallel) converts the reconstruction output into the flat training tree the network reads, and at the same time assembles the textual description of the network topology.

Topology string

The layout handed to YMultiLayerPerceptron is assembled from three groups: inputs s1[0..6], s2[0..6] — fourteen sensor-plane coordinates; outputs Stave[l], Chip[l], ChipID[l] for seven layers — twenty-one addressing branches that route each hit to its sensor network; and an addition group carrying the quantities the cost needs but the network does not consume. The output group is addressing, not regression targets: the quantity being minimized is the track fit, not a labelled truth.

The addition group has grown from eight branches to nine, and its content has changed character. The 2024 group was X1, X2, X3, P1, P2, P3, evno, ntracks — vertex position and event momentum. The 2025 group is X1, X2, X3, pt, eta, charge, phi, evno, ntracks: the three event-momentum branches are replaced by four per-track kinematic branches. This is what makes the momentum-seeded circle fit and the momentum-weighted vertexer of Section 06 possible, and it is why YNeuron::fLFEaddition was widened from eight formula slots to nine.

Selection and normalization

  • Events with fewer than nTrackMax = 8 tracks are skipped — the cost is defined on a multi-prong event sharing one vertex, and the vertexer needs enough pairs to survive losing a track or two to its quality gate.
  • Events with more than one associated vertex in the time frame (nvtx > 1) are skipped, to avoid pile-up contaminating the vertex constraint.
  • For every one of the 24 120 sensors the active-area corners are transformed (row, col) = (0,0) and (512, 1024) through LToG then GToS, giving per-sensor minima, maxima and spans in s1 and s2. These define the input normalization; the network sees coordinates shifted to [−0.5, +0.5] via fNorm_shift = 0.5.
  • The hit position is taken from the stored sensor-frame triplet GToS(ChipID, s1, s2, s3) rather than re-derived from the pixel address LToG(ChipID, row + 0.5, col + 0.5). The pixel-centre route is still present as commented code. This removes one quantization of the measurement and is the reason the re-tuned intrinsic resolutions of Section 07 come out as low as they do on the inner layers.
  • An event-weight hook (GetEventWeight, based on the z acceptance of each layer) exists but the cut on it is commented out in the current version.

The result is MLPTrain/XXXXtrain/XXXXtrain.root containing InputTree. The training and test samples are then carved out of it by event number: evno % 10 ∈ [0, 6) is the training set and [6, 8) is the test set used inside the job. The remaining 20 % is attached to neither TEventList and is held in reserve — it never enters the loss, the line search or the early-stop logic.

Two levels of validation

The evno % 10 split is the in-job control on a single prepared sample: it is what the loss curve compares, and it is what detects a step over-fitting to the subset of sensors that happen to be well populated in that sample. It is deliberately small, since every event withheld is one not contributing to a 24 120-sensor gradient.

Genuine validation is done separately, on an independently prepared sample that the campaign has never seen — a different run or fill, reconstructed and converted through the same upstream chain into its own XXXXinput.root. That pool is evaluated by running a job with nEPOCH = 0 against the trained weight set, which performs the epoch −1 pass only and produces the full residual and monitoring output without updating anything. Judging an alignment on the in-job test slice alone would be judging it on the same detector conditions it was fitted under.

Section 05Network topology

There is no deep network anywhere in this module. The model is 24 120 copies of a single affine map, plus a hierarchy of shared units that is built but currently not trained.

The detector-unit hierarchy

Alongside the flat sensor collection, BuildDetectorUnitNetwork constructs an eight-level tree of YSensorCorrection objects that share parameters across increasingly fine groupings:

This is the natural home for a coarse-to-fine alignment: releasing level 1 first fixes the three barrel groups as rigid bodies, then progressively finer levels absorb the remaining structure. The tree is built, its update states are initialized, and its weights are dumped every epoch into weightsDU*.txt.

Active configuration

In the present version the hierarchy is still inert. Every SetUpdateState(true) call in SetDetectorUnitNetworkUpdateStateUser remains commented out, and the learning method selected in run_train_circle.C is kStochastic, which updates the per-sensor networks directly rather than through the hierarchy. The hierarchical path (kBatchDetectorUnitUser, ComputeDCDwDetectorUnit, MLP_BatchDetectorUnit) is complete but not exercised. The weightsDU files are consequently written and re-read as identity carriers.

Section 06Adaptive vertex estimation

This is the substantive addition of the 2025 version. The vertex that anchors the track fit is no longer taken from the reconstruction and held fixed. It is recomputed from the ITS tracks themselves, through the alignment currently under training, once per event and once per epoch — so the constraint improves as the alignment improves instead of pinning it to whatever the upstream vertexer produced under the old geometry.

Why the fixed vertex was a problem

The 2024 cost used BeamCenter(X1, X2, X3), the per-event reconstruction vertex, as an eighth point in the circle fit, and protected itself with a hard guard: any track whose fitted transverse vertex landed more than 0.1 cm from the beam axis was dropped. That arrangement has two failure modes. The reconstruction vertex was itself produced under the starting geometry, so a coherent misalignment the module is trying to remove is also baked into the constraint that is supposed to remove it — the alignment is anchored to part of its own error. And a fixed positional guard is a cut on the wrong quantity: it rejects a well-measured low-momentum track that genuinely scattered just as readily as a high-momentum track that is genuinely inconsistent with its neighbours.

Both call sites for the fix already existed in 2024 — UpdateVertexByAlignment() appears twice in that source, commented out in both places. In this version both are live, and the 0.1 cm guard is the line that is now commented out.

The pair estimator

VertexerFit takes two tracks reduced to lines P + tQ. If the two directions disagree in azimuth by more than 90° one is flipped, so a pair is always combined in the same sense; if the lines are parallel to within |Qk × Qj| < 10−10 the pair is discarded as degenerate. The parameters of closest approach follow from the usual 2×2 system in Q·Q, giving one point on each line; their unweighted mean Vjk is the pair's vertex estimate.

The precision attached to that estimate is where the physics enters. A pair's uncertainty is anisotropic — two tracks crossing at a shallow angle localize the vertex well perpendicular to their common direction and badly along it — so the covariance is built diagonal in a frame aligned with the pair bisector and then rotated into the global frame, C⁻¹ → Rᵀ C⁻¹ R. The three diagonal widths scale as 1 / ⟨p⟩ with the mean pair momentum, and their coefficients are functions of the opening angle held as TF1 objects:

The high power of cos α is doing one specific job: it is flat across most of the angular range and turns up sharply only as the pair becomes collinear, which is exactly where the closest-approach construction degenerates. The _L0 set is used for the first round, when the lines are tangents at the innermost layer and the geometric lever arm is long; the _V set, whose turn-up is sharper still, is used once the lines are tangents at the vertex estimate itself.

VertexerCheck repeats the same construction after V0 is known and accumulates the pair χ2 and its degrees of freedom, so each round reports a normalized quality of its own estimate. Those numbers are printed per epoch under UPDATEVERTEXDEBUG and are the direct diagnostic of whether the vertexer is converging.

The track quality gate

After the third round, every track is propagated to the current estimate by TrackVertexQualityEstimator. The helix is intersected with the line from the vertex to the circle centre to find the point of closest approach, the momentum vector is reconstructed there from pT, η, φ and the charge, and the result is handed to YImpactParameter, which returns the transverse and longitudinal impact parameters with respect to the estimated vertex. The score is

p · |d| / 40 µmthe deviation actually cut on
5QUALITY_VERTEXING — per-track limit
10QUALITY_TRACKVERTEX — event limit
2bad tracks tolerated per event

Multiplying by the momentum is what makes this a cut on inconsistency rather than on displacement. Multiple scattering broadens the impact parameter as 1/p, so p·|d| is approximately scattering-invariant: a 500 MeV/c track and a 5 GeV/c track are held to the same standard of agreement with the vertex, expressed in units of 40 µm · GeV/c. A track that fails has every pair it belongs to removed from goodPairs, in both directions of the matrix; more than two failures and the event is abandoned.

The vertex is re-estimated, not differentiated

VERTEX_DERIVATIVES is FALSE. Within one gradient evaluation the vertex is treated as a constant: ∂C/∂w is taken with the vertex point held fixed, even though that point depends on the same weights through the tracks that produced it. The blocks that would carry the extra term are present in CalculateEventDcdw and are compiled out.

This is a deliberate fixed-point scheme rather than an oversight: estimate the vertex at the current weights, take a step holding it fixed, re-estimate at the new weights. It is correct at convergence — where the vertex stops moving, the neglected term vanishes — and it keeps the per-event gradient exactly as cheap as it was in 2024. The cost is that the loss being descended is not, strictly, the loss whose gradient is being followed while the vertex is still moving, which is one more reason the epoch guard of Section 08 was tightened in this version.

Section 07The cost function

A cost is defined per track, summed over the tracks of an event, normalized by the event's degrees of freedom, and summed over events. The per-track chain runs in GetCost_Beam_CircleFit, and it begins with a vertex point that Section 06 has already established.

Two thresholds, two passes

The quality gate is deliberately not the same in the two places a track is examined. When the cost is evaluated, a track is admitted if it has at least five valid hits, a total Σχ² no greater than TrackRejection = 110, and no single coordinate beyond RANGE_CHI_IB / OB = 100. When the gradient is accumulated in CalculateEventDcdw, two further conditions apply: the track must have a hit on all seven layers, and every coordinate must satisfy the far tighter RANGE_CHI_IB_TRAINING = 5 in the inner barrel or RANGE_CHI_OB_TRAINING = 9 in the outer.

The asymmetry is the point. The loss is a measurement of the whole sample and should not be silently computed on a cherry-picked subset, so it keeps the loose thresholds. The update is a step in parameter space and a single grossly inconsistent hit can drag a sensor a long way, so it only listens to tracks that are already well described. In 2024 both passes used the loose thresholds alone; these training-only limits are new.

From track cost to event cost

GetCost_Beam sums the track costs of an event and divides by ndf = (2(nlayer + 1) − 4) × nused + 1 = 12 nused + 1, where nused is the number of tracks that survived. Two coordinates per hit across seven layers plus the vertex point give sixteen constraints; four helix parameters are fitted; the extra unit accounts for the shared vertex. If every track of an event is rejected — or if the vertexer of Section 06 refused the event — the event itself is counted as lost and contributes zero. The per-event normalized cost is also filled into hEventChi2TOT, which is new in this version.

The charge-symmetry monitor

For each of twenty azimuthal patches the module accumulates Σ q · pT over the tracks falling in that patch. A residual net charge–momentum asymmetry in a φ sector is the signature of a curvature bias — a sagitta-like weak mode that leaves the track χ² almost untouched while systematically distorting the momentum scale. The positive and negative track counts per patch are printed every epoch, with the patch ratios and their difference.

Changed behavior

In the 2024 release this quantity was added to the reported loss, making it a penalty term in the objective. In this version all four Init_training_E += …_chargesym and Init_test_E += …_chargesym statements are commented out. The asymmetry is accumulated, printed and plotted, but it no longer enters the loss and therefore no longer influences the weight update. Treat it as a diagnostic, not as a constraint.

Section 08The optimization loop

YMultiLayerPerceptron::Train(nEpoch, "graph, text, update=1") holds the loop. Its structure is four nested scales, and each boundary is where something is written to disk.

Epoch −1: the baseline

Before the first update the module performs a full evaluation pass labelled epoch −1. It calls PrepareDumpResiduals and ComputeTrackProfile, evaluates the training and test costs, and writes Residual_Epoch_At_-1.root, NTracksBySensor, and the sensor and half-stave track-occupancy plots. This baseline is what every later epoch is compared against, and it is also the pass that populates the update-sensor list. Setting nEPOCH = 0 makes the job stop after it — the standard way to produce monitoring for an existing weight set without training.

Update-sensor list

UpdateSensorsList.txt carries one line per ChipID with the number of tracks seen and a status flag. Its behavior is set at compile time by MONITORONLYUPDATES_MODE:

ModeBehavior
−1Flexible — the list is regenerated from the occupancy at every epoch.
0Generate — the list is produced from the epoch −1 pass and the job returns without training.
1Fixed (current) — the list is loaded from the previous step and re-emitted unchanged, so the set of sensors under correction is stable across a campaign.

The occupancy threshold Min_Cluster_by_Sensor is 0 in the committed configuration, so no sensor is excluded on statistics alone; the list is inherited rather than recomputed.

Learning rate and schedule

The nominal learning rate is built from a target resolution rather than tuned as an abstract number: η = UpdateConstant × UpdateScale × (DETRES × 10−4)² with 0.05, 10−8 and DETRES = 1000 µm, giving η = 5 × 10−12 in the squared-centimeter units of the cost — an order of magnitude smaller than the 2024 value, consistent with a sharper resolution model and a vertex that is itself moving between epochs. YAlignment::TrainMLP additionally sets τ = 100 e−0.1 s, annealing the line-search bracket expansion factor across the campaign; τ is consumed only by the line search of kSteepestDescent and is therefore inactive under the stochastic method selected in the current version.

Section 09Weights to alignment parameters

CalculateSCWeights is where the module stops being a neural network and becomes an alignment. It runs per sensor, inside the weight update, and performs three operations in sequence. This machinery is unchanged from the 2024 release.

  1. Assemble the geometric context. The sensor's local-to-global matrix is decomposed into a rotation Rgeom and a translation Tgeom. Two constant matrices, a signed permutation P carrying the mounting direction and an axis-order matrix O, map between the O2 local frame and the sensor frame used here. A diagonal matrix D1 = diag(1/d1, 1/d2, 1) with d1 = 2.991253 cm and d2 = 1.373568 cm undoes the input normalization, and a small offset D2 absorbs the sensor-frame origin shift.
  2. Differentiate the rigid-body map analytically. For each of α, β, γ the derivative of the rotation matrix is written out in closed form and conjugated into the network's parameter space, giving ∂wij/∂α and ∂bi/∂α and their counterparts. The translation derivatives are simply the columns of RgeomPO transposed. Contracting these with the accumulated ∂C/∂w yields ∂C/∂α through ∂C/∂T3 exactly, with no finite differences anywhere.
  3. Update and write back. The six geometric parameters are advanced along their own gradients, each scaled by its entry in the gate vectors Update_aR[3] and Update_aT[3]. Setting an entry to zero holds that degree of freedom fixed for the run without changing anything else, which is the mechanism behind a staged schedule — translations first, rotations released later, or a subset pinned while a weak mode is investigated. The network weights are then recomputed from the updated geometry so that the two descriptions never drift apart. Both are stored: each line of weights.txt is ChipID, five neuron offsets, six synapse weights, aR[0..2] and aT[0..2] — eighteen columns.
Why this matters

Because the mapping is exact and bidirectional, the learned object is a rigid-body alignment by construction and not merely something that resembles one. A per-sensor affine correction left unconstrained would have nine free parameters and could absorb shear and scale that no physical mounting can produce; here it has exactly the six of a rigid body. The network is the numerical vehicle, the geometry is the model.

NetworkParameters/weights.txt in the repository is the deployed parameter set; weights_np.txt is the same file reduced by howto.txt to its first twelve columns, that is, the network description without the geometric one.

Reading the vertexer output

The natural first check on a new sample is the fraction of events lost before any track cost is computed. Two counters matter and they fail for different reasons: an event lost to Num_Of_Failed_Tracks > 0 means a tracker fit did not converge, which points at the input — missing charge, a bad momentum, hits outside their sensor bounds. An event lost to Num_Of_Bad_Tracks > 2 means the fits converged but the tracks do not agree on where the vertex is, which points at the alignment itself and should shrink as the campaign proceeds. If it does not shrink, the constraint is fighting the fit rather than guiding it.

Offline analysis

monitor/analysis.sh launches five ROOT jobs in parallel against Residual_Monitor_Epoch_At_-1.root:

  • CheckQuality — curvature against charge and pT in two dimensions and per φ patch, the total χ² distribution, and the charge-symmetry monitor.
  • ImpactParams — impact-parameter and vertex distributions.
  • Residual — residuals by layer, repeated at pT thresholds of 0, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 GeV/c, since a residual that only closes at high pT indicates a scattering-dominated rather than geometric effect.
  • Residual2D and Profile — two-dimensional residual maps and their profiles.

Separately, YAlignment::ReconstructGeometries(res_level) samples each sensor's active area on a 2res_level × 2res_level+1 grid and writes three geometries — ideal, target and corrected — into Geom/XXXXGeom.root as TNtuples indexed by the full addressing chain. This is the direct route to a sensor-by-sensor displacement map.

Section 11Step chaining

A campaign converges across steps. Three files carry state forward; everything else is regenerated — including, now, the vertex of every event.

Step 0 is the exception: fPrevWeights is empty, so the networks are randomized by Init_Randomize and its sensor-level counterparts, and no update-sensor list is inherited. From step 1 onward the three files above are loaded before the epoch −1 baseline is evaluated, which is why that baseline is directly comparable to the previous step's final epoch.

Section 14Notes for alignment groups

For readers who align other trackers: this section places the method against the two families most groups already use, and states plainly which parts are conventional and which are not.

What is conventional

  • The objective is a track-fit χ² with residuals in the sensor plane, normalized by a resolution model that adds intrinsic and multiple-scattering terms in quadrature, with the scattering term scaling as 1/(BR).
  • The alignable object is a rigid body with six degrees of freedom per sensor.
  • A vertex constraint from multi-prong events supplies the information that a track fit alone cannot: without it, coherent radial and longitudinal modes are unconstrained.
  • Outliers are removed by hard cuts on the normalized residual and on the total track χ².

The vertex constraint is self-consistent, not external

This is the point of the method most likely to be transferable, and the one most likely to be contentious. A vertex constraint taken from the reconstruction carries the misalignment of the geometry that produced it. If that geometry is the one being corrected, part of the error is being used as its own reference, and the coherent modes the vertex is supposed to fix are exactly the ones it will be least able to see. Re-deriving the vertex from the same tracks, at the current alignment, removes that circularity at the price of a fixed-point iteration whose convergence is empirical rather than proved.

Two properties make it tractable here. The estimator is closed-form — pairwise closest approach with an inverse-covariance mean, no minimizer — so running it on every event of every epoch costs little against the track fits already being performed. And the quality gate is on p·|d| rather than on |d|, which makes it approximately scattering-invariant and therefore a genuine consistency test rather than a disguised momentum cut. A group considering the same move should budget for the second point in particular: with a plain distance cut, the vertexer preferentially discards soft tracks, which are also the tracks that populate the outer layers most densely, and the alignment quietly acquires a radius-dependent bias.

What differs from a Millepede-style global fit

  • No global matrix. Alignment parameters are never assembled into a single linear system. The gradient with respect to each sensor's parameters is accumulated independently and applied immediately, so the memory footprint is O(Nsensor) rather than O((6Nsensor)²). For 24 120 sensors that is the difference between a few megabytes and a dense matrix of order 1010 elements. The price is that inter-sensor correlations are not solved in one shot; they are recovered by iterating steps.
  • Iteration replaces inversion. Convergence is empirical and is monitored through the loss curve and the residual distributions, not certified by a covariance matrix. The module produces no parameter uncertainties; a Millepede solution does.
  • Weak modes are attacked by the vertex, not by constraint equations. There is no explicit set of linear constraints fixing the global frame; the reference-sensor mechanism (SetSplitReferenceSensor) exists but is disabled. The frame is held by the vertex constraint, and where a campaign calls for it, by gating individual degrees of freedom through Update_aR and Update_aT. Note that in this version the charge–momentum asymmetry term, which in 2024 penalized a sagitta-like distortion directly in the cost, has been demoted to a monitor — so that weak mode is now watched rather than suppressed.
  • Robustness by rejection rather than by down-weighting. There is no M-estimator; a track either enters the cost in full or not at all. This is simple and fast but discards information at the edges of the residual distribution.

What differs from a local iterative alignment

A local method aligns one sensor at a time against tracks fitted from the others, and must be iterated to propagate information. Here all 24 120 sensors are updated in the same pass from the same track fits, so information propagates across the detector within a single epoch. The construction is closer to a simultaneous gradient descent on the full parameter vector than to a sequence of local solves — the network formalism is what makes the bookkeeping of that gradient tractable.

Transferable pieces

  • The analytic weight-to-geometry Jacobian is the reusable core. Any alignment expressed as a per-sensor affine map can be constrained to rigid-body motion the same way, with the same guarantee that the fitted object stays physical.
  • The self-consistent vertex and its momentum-weighted quality gate are detector-independent. Both need only track parameters and charge, and the pair covariance model — anisotropic in the pair frame, scaling as 1/⟨p⟩, turning up sharply for collinear pairs — is a compact way to express what a two-track vertex actually knows.
  • The split thresholds between the cost pass and the update pass: measure the loss on everything that is admissible, but take steps only from tracks that are already well described. This costs nothing and prevents a single bad hit from moving a sensor.
  • The learning-rate backtracking guard — revert, divide the step by √10, retry, abandon after four attempts — is a compact substitute for a full line search when the cost evaluation is expensive.
  • The step archive convention, in which each iteration is a self-contained tarball with its own weights, residuals and gradients, makes a campaign auditable after the fact rather than only while it runs.

Honest limitations of the present version

  • The vertex is re-estimated but not differentiated. The scheme is a fixed-point iteration, exact only at convergence, and its stability is enforced by the epoch guard rather than by construction.
  • The pair covariance coefficients are hard-coded constants fitted on one sample. They are not derived per run, and nothing in the job checks that they still describe the sample in front of it.
  • The charge–momentum asymmetry is no longer penalized, so no term in the objective opposes a sagitta-like weak mode.
  • The detector-unit hierarchy that would enable a coarse-to-fine schedule is built but not enabled.
  • No parameter uncertainties or correlations are produced.
  • Configuration lives in preprocessor defines, so a scan over cut values requires recompilation and cannot be varied within a campaign.
